## **Understanding How Cashback Sites Actually Work**

Before we compare platforms, let's clear up how cashback sites make money in the first place—because once you understand that, everything else makes sense.

When you buy something through a cashback platform, the retailer pays that platform a commission for sending them a customer. Simple enough. But here's where platforms diverge wildly: some platforms keep 30-40% of that commission for themselves and pass the rest to you. Others keep almost nothing and pass nearly everything back to members.

TopCashback falls into the second category. They pass 100% of earned commission back to members. That's not hype. That's their actual business model. Rakuten, by comparison, takes a percentage cut from what you earn. When you see a 3% offer on Rakuten and a 4% offer on TopCashback for the same store, the difference isn't always in the store's generosity—it's often in what the platform decided to keep.

The mechanics work the same across platforms: you sign up, activate an offer or use a link before shopping, complete your purchase, and the cashback deposits into your account (either pending first, then payable after tracking). From there, you can withdraw via PayPal, bank transfer, or gift cards depending on what the platform supports.

Where it gets interesting is in the details. Some platforms have monthly redemptions. Others let you cash out anytime. Some have minimum thresholds that lock your money away. These small differences compound over time, especially if you're a heavy online shopper.
